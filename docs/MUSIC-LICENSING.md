## Clearance required before public use

Pairing copyrighted music with a trailer, episode, film, game, advertisement, social video, or other visual work normally requires at least:

1. **Synchronization license** — permission for the underlying composition (music and lyrics) from every applicable publisher, administrator, or songwriter.
2. **Master-use license** — permission for the specific released recording from its owner or authorized representative.
3. **Adaptation approval** — express permission for lyric changes, narrative rewriting, interpolation, translation, remixing, or other derivative treatment.
4. **Name, likeness, and endorsement review** — separate written approval before marketing suggests that Ren or any collaborator participates in, presents, scores, or endorses the project.
5. **Additional media rights** — game, soundtrack-album, trailer, paid social, festival, platform, territory, term, options, and exclusivity must be stated in the request and contract.

A license from one participant may not clear a collaboration. “Blind Eyed” and “What Went Wrong” involve Sam Tompkins; “How to Be Me” and “Chalk Outlines” involve Chinchilla. Confirm all ownership shares and approvals track by track.

Noncommercial status, attribution, a “fair use” label, an unsuccessful attempt to contact a rightsholder, or availability on a streaming/social platform is not permission.

## Contact and verification route

1. Retain a music supervisor or entertainment attorney to prepare the request and verify chain of title.
2. Approach Ren's management and **The Other Songs** as the initial routing point for applicable Ren recordings and publishing inquiries.
3. Ask the representative to identify, in writing, every composition owner/administrator, master owner, approval party, ownership percentage, and any samples or third-party material.
4. For compositions that a representative identifies as administered by Universal Music Publishing Group, use UMPG's licensing channel. Do not assume The Other Songs' UMPG partnership covers every Ren song.
5. For collaborations, contact the co-writers' and featured performers' authorized representatives as directed by the ownership response.
6. Obtain executed licenses before editing music into any public proof of concept, trailer, episode, game build, advertisement, or fundraising presentation distributed beyond a controlled private review.

### Information to include in a request

- Project title and concise synopsis
- Exact track and exact recording/version
- Scene description and sensitive-content context
- Duration and type of use (background, visual vocal, titles, trailer, etc.)
- Media, platforms, territory, term, release window, and exclusivity
- Paid advertising and social-media use
- Game/interactivity and soundtrack-album rights, if applicable
- Editing, looping, lyric display, subtitles, translation, or adaptation requested
- Budget and most-favored-nations terms, if proposed
- Credit language
- A request for express permission concerning the “Violet” title/story association

## Public and private language

Until rights and participation are secured, use:

> **Development music reference only. No music rights secured. No affiliation with or endorsement by Ren or any other artist or rightsholder.**

Do not use “music by Ren,” “Ren soundtrack,” “official collaboration,” or similar language.

A private pitch may identify desired licensing targets if it carries the notice above and does not contain the recordings or lyrics. Public repository materials should link to official artist releases rather than upload, reproduce, transcribe, sample, or embed copied audio/video assets.
