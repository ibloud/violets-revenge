## Signature field hockey stick — owner-directed design (16 November 2026)

**Owner-defined identity:** Violet carries a **field hockey stick**, not a sword, dagger, ice-hockey stick, umbrella or scythe. It must be visible and recognizable in every full-body *character generation/reference sheet* featuring her signature prop. Her existing hockey-stick loadout, #88 and forensic identity are preserved. This adds the missing motivation and movement reference; it does not revise combat mechanics.

- **Prop form:** Retain the recognizable curved wooden/field-hockey head, shaft, grip, and correct scale. Do not turn the head into a metal blade, exaggerated axe, cane hook, ice-hockey blade or an umbrella canopy. Its original design and exact head geometry require owner art review; depict the existing established stick unless a new prop design is approved.
- **Movement signature:** Owner-directed inspiration from classic Hollywood umbrella/cane dance staging (carriage, poised turns, clean arcs, prop-as-partner), combined with the precision, collective rhythm and body-percussion language of HBCU step-show traditions and hip-hop. These are **distinct creative and cultural influences**, not a claimed straight historical evolution. A solo Violet routine may evoke disciplined rhythm; do not label an invented movement as an authentic Divine Nine step or reproduce a particular organization's step, chant, calls, colors or insignia.
- **Emotional function:** The stick is first an object with a life and practiced rhythm, and only in later contexts a weapon. Its movement can move from measured, almost playful grace to a controlled investigative or confrontational posture. Keep a purposeful character choice rather than a generic slasher flourish.
- **Research boundary:** The Hockey Museum documents historical hedgerow and later field-hockey head forms; it does not establish a sword/dagger ancestry. Step Afrika! documents stepping as a percussive form associated with African-American fraternities and sororities. The National Library of Wales documents the medieval White Book of Rhydderch and its transmission. **None of these sources establishes a historical link between Welsh manuscript culture, field hockey sticks and HBCU stepping.** The cross-era comparison is an original story interpretation, not a historical claim.
- **Canon gate:** The prop identity and owner-supplied artistic inspirations are documented here. Exact dance choreography, heritage/backstory, and the scene described in `docs/VIOLET_STICK_SCENE_TREATMENT.md` remain **proposed, not approved plot canon** until reviewed under `docs/STORY-BIBLE.md`. Do not imply Violet was a member of an HBCU or Black Greek-letter organization without a separate owner decision.
