# Sebastian and the Political Conflict

Sebastian rejects what royalty represents. His desire to become royal is instrumental: he seeks access to power in order to free people from the structure that concentrates it. He and Lady Violet discover that they want the same outcome, but do not bear the same consequences.

## Unequal freedom

| Sebastian | Lady Violet |
|---|---|
| Free to rebel openly | Bound by responsibility for those who may suffer |
| Can leave the institution | Controls resources the kingdom needs |
| Risks himself through refusal | Risks a population through refusal |
| Sees the moral clarity of opposition | Must manage the material consequences of transition |

Their dramatic question is whether they can dismantle the same system while sharing the cost of resistance.

## Design implications

- Rebellion and responsibility must both produce meaningful choices.
- Visible defiance is not automatically more ethical than strategic compromise.
- Violet's resources must become collective capacity, not a prize transferred between men.
- Sebastian's mobility should help create an alternative system, not simply permit escape.
- The King's knowledge of the imbalance complicates him without converting coerced marriage into consent.

## Collaboration question

External perspective—especially from male readers and performers—may test whether Sebastian reads as psychologically credible rather than an idealized answer to Violet. Consultation does not transfer authorship or canon authority. Scope, credit, rights, and compensation status require written agreement.
