- **Old Hollywood dancer with umbrella — owner inspiration.** Translate poised posture, sweeping arcs, wrist articulation, silhouette, counterbalance, theatrical pauses and partnership with the prop into *original* motion. The stick remains a stick, never morphs into an umbrella.
- **Hip-hop and HBCU stepping — owner inspiration.** Explore weight transfers, grounded footwork, rhythmic stamps, precise stops, syncopation and call-and-response *as movement principles*. Stepping is a culturally specific, group-rooted body-percussion practice—not a generic combat style. Engage knowledgeable dancers/choreographers for any identifiable stepping sequence. - **Historical comparison, not ancestry:** Curved wooden sticks appear in the documented history of field hockey. The White Book of Rhydderch is a separate medieval Welsh textual source for the *theme of reconstruction*. No evidence in the cited material establishes field hockey's derivation from weapons, or a transmission from the White Book to hockey or stepping.

## Provenance and cultural research
- [The Hockey Museum / FIH — Changing the Game](https://www.fih.hockey/general/news/the-hockey-museum-changing-the-game): historical stick families include hedgerow, English-head, Indian-head, composite and metal. Supports a curved wooden-stick *design history*, not sword ancestry.
- [Step Afrika! — Repertoire](https://www.stepafrika.org/repertoire/): stepping's Black fraternity/sorority history; footsteps, clapping, spoken rhythms, evolving influences.
- [National Library of Wales — White Book of Rhydderch](https://www.library.wales/discover-learn/digital-exhibitions/manuscripts/the-middle-ages/white-book-of-rhydderch/): manuscript contents, copying and loss, useful as a metaphor for interpretation and reconstruction—not a source on sports or stepping.
