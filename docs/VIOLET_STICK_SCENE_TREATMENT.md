# Violet's stick — “The Measure of What Remains”
**Scene-treatment proposal, NOT APPROVED STORY CANON · 16 November 2026**

**Creative question:** Why does Violet carry a field hockey stick? Its importance is not merely that it becomes a weapon. It links the curve of a utilitarian object to rehearsed movement, personal memory, rhythmic discipline and the choice to investigate before judging.

**Existing canon constraints:** Violet is a methodical forensics examiner and later executioner; the three-act structure remains The Examiner → The Attack (off-screen) → The Executioner. The attack must remain off-screen. Do not give the Guilty names or personal redemption arcs, change existing mechanics, or assert a confirmed biographical link to an HBCU, Greek-letter organization, dance company or medieval Wales. Use the existing props/visual authority in `docs/CHARACTER-BIBLE.md`.

## Treatment — a quiet discovery, not a new origin myth

**Location:** An archive/workroom, or a pre-existing research space to be selected for the story. No new geography is canonized.

1. **The object.** Violet sets her field hockey stick beside her investigation notes. Its curved head catches the light. To another character it might look like a weapon, but she examines the grain, balance, repaired marks and grip, treating it as evidence of prior use. Sound: a single grounded footfall, room tone.
2. **The memory in movement.** She lifts it by the shaft, briefly aligning her body with an old-Hollywood umbrella dancer's elegant silhouette—but there is no actual umbrella. A controlled sweep resolves into a precise stop. Her other foot answers with a syncopated step; the stick is never used to strike anyone. The gesture is original choreography, a contrast between performance, athletic training and forensic restraint.
3. **Listening to rhythm.** She lets two distinct influences coexist: theatrical prop choreography, and the grounded body percussion and shared discipline associated with hip-hop and HBCU stepping. Do not make these one false ancestral tradition. The beat grows from steps and controlled pauses, not a sampled or copied ceremonial step/chant. A cultural movement consultant should approve any explicitly identifiable stepping reference.
4. **A separate research question.** Nearby, research notes on the *White Book of Rhydderch* show a copied text altered by time, custodians and missing pages. The page is NOT evidence of stick-and-ball ancestry. Its function is thematic: what survives, who names it, and how a later reader mistakes the fragment for the whole. The author may instead locate this research in another character's parallel investigation; **direct crossover with PIXIE and the grad student is not authorized by this proposal.**
5. **The discovery.** Violet places the stick across the notes, carefully without damaging documents. The curve makes an imperfect frame around a missing line. She adjusts her stance, stops the beat and writes: **“The object remembers a use. It does not tell me who gave it meaning.”** This line is an *original proposed draft*, not fixed dialogue. She chooses evidence-gathering over premature judgment.
6. **Exit image.** The stick rests visibly against the table, not staged as a sword. A faint toe-tap keeps time. Cut before action; no attack is shown or implied as a historical re-enactment.

## Why the scene earns its place
- **Character motivation:** Reframes an existing signature prop with Violet's interiority and practiced control, without reversing her established story.
- **Visual continuity:** Defines the recurring hook of curved field-hockey head, poised sweep, rhythmic grounded pause and forensic inspection, for concept artists and animators.
- **Discovery motif:** The White Book provides a *separate*, source-backed example of manuscripts surviving as incomplete records; it mirrors Violet's method of reconstruction but is not the prop's provenance.
- **Performance vocabulary:** Old-Hollywood umbrella choreography and Black collegiate stepping/hip-hop traditions supply two distinct inspirations. Their formal histories and cultural ownership must be acknowledged instead of collapsing them into one fabricated origin.
